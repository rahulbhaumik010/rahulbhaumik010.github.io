Overview

Designer and HCI researcher exploring how form, surface and material shape the way we touch, press and interact with technology.

Rahul Bhaumik is a designer and researcher working at the intersection of human-computer interaction, extended reality, soft interfaces, and design pedagogy. He is a researcher and member of the Media Interaction Lab at the Free University of Bozen-Bolzano (unibz), Italy. He completed his PhD at unibz in 2026 with the thesis Affordances of Soft Interfaces: A Design and Fabrication Approach, which investigates soft, passive interfaces — from textiles to 3D-printed smart structures that sense pressure and deformation — for everyday and automotive interactions. In 2025 he spent six months as a Visiting Researcher at the Human-Computer Interaction Institute (HCII) at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh.

Trained as an architect and product designer, his work focuses on the tactile, material, and experiential dimensions of human-technology relationships — drawing upon emerging fabrication technologies and morphological principles to create interfaces that are both functionally innovative and perceptually engaging. His work has appeared at ACM CHI and ACM AutomotiveUI and in IEEE Sensors Letters and Communications Engineering.

Before his doctoral studies, he was an Assistant Professor at the Faculty of Architecture & Design, PES University, Bangalore (2018–2023), where he taught and researched at the confluence of digital design tools, learning systems, and interaction design. He holds an M.Des in Product Design and Engineering from the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, and a B.Arch from the School of Planning and Architecture, New Delhi.

Doctoral thesis · 2026

Affordances of Soft Interfaces: A Design and Fabrication Approach

Degree

PhD in Computer Science
Free University of Bozen-Bolzano

Supervisors

Prof. Michael Haller
Prof. Antonella De Angeli

Awarded

12 June 2026

The thesis investigates how to design and fabricate soft, passive interfaces, and how their affordances emerge from geometry, surface and material. It spans textile interfaces and 3D-printed smart structures that detect touch and pressure while offering soft, deformable surfaces for everyday interaction — asking how an interface's overall form, the visual and tactile qualities of its surface, and the flexibility of its material shape the gestures people perform on it.

  1. FormInteracting with 3D surfaces

    Experiments on a touch-sensitive, three-dimensional middle console for cars, evaluating how surface geometry affects usability and which gestural patterns people perform on 3D interfaces.

    AutomotiveUI ’24 ↗

  2. SurfaceTextiles that guide touch

    How visual and tactile surface features guide on-surface gestures, explored through the design and prototyping of functional and non-functional textile-based interfaces.

  3. MaterialSoft 3D-printed sensing

    Deformable 3D-printed structures with ferromagnetic filaments that use inductive sensing for pressure and out-of-plane gestures — evaluated for mechanics, sensing performance and long-term stability, and demonstrated as controllers, weighing systems and smart shoes.

    IEEE Sens. Lett. ’25 ↗ CHI ’26 ↗
